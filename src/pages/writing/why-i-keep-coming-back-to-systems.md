## The interface is only the surface

Apps are interesting. The machinery underneath them is where I keep finding the questions I want to answer.

Processes, networks, storage, permissions, protocols and failure modes are not separate worlds. They are parts of a connected system, and a problem at one layer can show up as a confusing symptom at another.

## Systems reward patient questions

When something stops working, I want to know:

- What changed?
- What can I observe directly?
- Which component is responsible for the behaviour?
- What is the smallest test that can separate two explanations?
- How would I know that the fix actually worked?

These questions are useful whether I am tracing a network problem, reading a log, debugging an application, or thinking about security.

## Build, observe, explain

Building something forces a mental model to meet reality. Observing it reveals where the model was incomplete. Explaining the result in writing helps expose the parts I only thought I understood.

That is the cycle I want my projects and notes to show.

## Still learning

I am early in my career. I do not need to pretend that I know every layer already. I need to keep getting better at investigating systems, recording evidence, and correcting my assumptions when the evidence disagrees.
