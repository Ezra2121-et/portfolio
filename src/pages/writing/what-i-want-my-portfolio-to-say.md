## A portfolio is a signal

I do not want this site to shout that I am passionate, innovative, or a problem solver. Those words are cheap. The work should make the point.

A recruiter should be able to open a project, understand the problem in a minute, find the source code, and see what I actually contributed.

## Evidence beats adjectives

For each project, I want to make four things easy to find:

- **The problem:** Why did this need to exist?
- **My part:** What did I personally build, decide, test, or coordinate?
- **Evidence:** Code, screenshots, tests, logs, or a reproducible demo.
- **Limitations:** What does the project not prove yet?

That last item matters. A local prototype is not a hosted service, a passing health endpoint is not a production guarantee, and a list of tools is not the same as demonstrated skill.

## A personal corner of the internet

The project pages show what I build. The Now page shows what currently has my attention. The writing page keeps the small lessons that would otherwise disappear after a task is done.

Together, those pages should make it easier to understand not just what I know, but how I work through a problem.

## The rule

Everything on this site should earn its place. If an element does not help a visitor understand the work or the person behind it, it probably does not belong.
